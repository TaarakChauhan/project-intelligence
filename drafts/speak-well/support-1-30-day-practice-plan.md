## Draft article body (unpublished)

Tip lists feel useful until the week you need a talk. Then the tips sit in a notes app while you wing it. A practice plan works because it puts one small job on a calendar and forces a recording.

Speak Well already hosts a full 30 day plan. This article is the search facing version: why the plan works, how the four weeks stack, and how to keep the loop going when life gets busy.

### Why tip lists fail without a calendar

Most speaking advice is true and still unused. "Pause more." "Open with a story." "Know your audience." Without a day assignment and a finish line, those tips compete with email. A 30 day plan turns advice into reps. You pick a throughline, film a short take, and fix one thing tomorrow.

### The loop: one point, short rep, record, one fix

Use the same loop every practice day:

1. One clear point (a throughline sentence).
2. A short rep out loud (60 seconds to five minutes).
3. One recording on your phone.
4. One fix for the next day (fillers, opening, eye contact, structure).

Do not chase ten improvements at once. One fix compounds.

### Week 1: Foundations and nerves

Days 1 to 7 build mindset, throughline, basic structure, vocal variety, and a PREP drill. You end the week with a baseline video and a habit of cutting anything that does not support the point.

Pair with: [Foundations](/Speak-Well/learn/foundations.html) and [Nerves](/Speak-Well/learn/nerves.html). Full day list: [30 day plan](/Speak-Well/plan/30-day.html).

### Week 2: Craft and openings

Days 8 to 14 focus on openings, a short STAR story, sticky ideas, body language, and one complete informative talk on video. Checkpoint: stronger openings plus a full filmed talk you can score.

Pair with: [Structure](/Speak-Well/learn/structure.html), [Delivery](/Speak-Well/learn/delivery.html), and [Open strong](/Speak-Well/blog/open-strong.html).

### Week 3: Persuasion and live practice

Days 15 to 21 move into Monroe persuasion, Duarte contrast, a filmed ask, tough Q&A, peer or Toastmasters feedback, and impromptu drills. Checkpoint: a persuasive structure plus at least one live or peer feedback loop.

Pair with: [Monroe persuasion](/Speak-Well/blog/monroe-persuasion.html) and [Q&A](/Speak-Well/learn/qa.html).

### Week 4: Polish and systems

Days 22 to 30 build a signature talk, dress rehearsals, a nerves routine, a live or simulated delivery, and a sustainable weekly practice habit. The goal is not a perfect talk. The goal is a system you can keep: one filmed practice a week and peer feedback twice a month.

Pair with: [Practice systems](/Speak-Well/learn/practice.html) and [Delivery checklist](/Speak-Well/blog/delivery-checklist.html).

### Daily five minute micro drills

On busy days, keep the streak with a five minute drill:

- Random word: speak 60 seconds on a random object.
- One breath pause: insert a full silent pause at each period.
- First line: deliver your opening five times with energy.
- Filler hunt: 60 second talk, count um/uh, redo aiming for half.

### Start today

Open the live plan and do Day 1: watch one short talk on nerves or throughline, then write why you want to speak better in three sentences.
