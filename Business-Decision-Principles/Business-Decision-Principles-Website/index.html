Shared operating system

The seven-step decision loop

Judge decisions by the quality of the process and information available at the time — not only by the result. A good decision can lose; a weak one can win. The loop keeps framing, evidence, rights, and learning honest.

  1. 01

    Frame

    One sentence. Objective, constraints, owner, deadline, do-nothing path. Label one-way, two-way, or mixed.

  2. 02

    Options

    Preferred path, alternatives, a smaller test, and waiting. Do not confuse a favourite with the only option.

  3. 03

    Evidence

    Claims that must be true, evidence that would update belief, source quality, and downside if wrong.

  4. 04

    Decision rights

    Who recommends, contributes, decides, executes, approves, and reviews. More voices ≠ more accountability.

  5. 05

    Commit

    Decision, rationale, boundaries, next action, dissent heard. Disagree-and-commit means dissent before the call.

  6. 06

    Observe

    Leading indicators and cash, customer, and people signals. Short feedback loops for uncertain moves.

  7. 07

    Review

    Process, forecast misses, luck, one improvement. Reopen only with new evidence or a pre-defined trigger.

01 · Foundations

Five foundations of better judgment

Before specialist VC, angel, owner, or board technique — build the shared vocabulary. Ceremony should match reversibility; models should change the plan or be dropped.

01

Outcome vs process

Separate decision quality from outcome quality. Ask what you knew then — do not rewrite the forecast to fit the result.

02

One-way & two-way doors

Two-way doors: bounded experiments. One-way doors: slow down, invite dissent, model downside, get the right approvals.

03

Mental models

Expected value, inversion, premortem, second-order effects, OODA, regret minimisation, margin of safety — as lenses, not décor.

04

Journals & calibration

Record assumptions, ranges, and review triggers before acting. Calibration is a long-run property across a set of forecasts.

05

Bias safeguards

Confirmation, anchoring, availability, sunk cost, overconfidence, groupthink — design the environment, not a character diagnosis.

Role paths

Choose your lens

Same operating system — different constraints. Capital allocators, operators, and directors face distinct liquidity, authority, and emotional stakes.

Venture capital

Portfolio underwriting under uncertainty — not a product beauty contest. Ask both “Is this a good company?” and “Can this become a fund-relevant outcome at this price, ownership, stage, and reserve plan?”

  • Funnel: screen → deep dive → partner review → memo → IC
  • Diligence domains: financial, commercial, product, team, legal, ESG
  • Evidence changes by stage — pre-seed through growth
  • Red flags as investigation prompts; memos as falsifiable records

Angel investors

Personal capital means personal liquidity, concentration, time, relationships, and emotional stakes. Name the objective and write a mandate before FOMO or friendship overrides it.

  • Five checks: founder-market fit, timing/wedge, model integrity, references, terms
  • Light diligence: 48-hour screen, then a one-week deeper pass if the thesis survives
  • Size cheques from amounts that can be lost without changing essential life plans
  • Diversification reduces single-deal pressure — it does not guarantee returns

Owners & founders

Make recurring choices visible, owned, and reviewable. Maintain a weekly decision queue. Use experiments for two-way doors and short memos for one-way doors.

  • Allocation: hiring, pricing, build vs buy, pivot — each with clear alternatives
  • Raising capital is a strategic decision, not a validation ceremony
  • Cash survival: rolling forecasts, pre-agreed triggers, early action over panic
  • Disagree-and-commit with one accountable decider and a review trigger

Boards & governance

Directors generally owe duties to the corporation. Care means informed oversight; loyalty means good faith and managing conflicts. Educational overview only — not legal advice.

  • Govern purpose, CEO oversight, strategy/risk, major capital — do not become shadow management
  • Business judgment is a process concept, not immunity from bad faith or conflicts
  • Canada and Delaware are not interchangeable; analyse under applicable law
  • Conflicted transactions: disclose early, document alternatives, never backfill process

07 · Worksheets

Six tools you can copy today

Templates turn principles into muscle memory. Use them before advocacy pressure rises — and at review, when honesty is hardest.

Decision journal

Owner, deadline, alternatives, assumptions, ranges, disconfirming evidence, kill trigger, review date.

VC diligence checklist

Domains, evidence quality, confidence, downside if false, and the next falsifiable test.

Angel scorecard

Red / amber / green across ownership, cash, customers, IP, team, terms, and compliance.

One-way-door premortem

Imagine failure, list plausible causes, leading indicators, and mitigations before commitment.

Investment memo outline

Thesis, facts vs claims vs assumptions, scenarios, risks, recommendation, dissent, open items.

Weekly decision review

Owner cadence: what was decided, what was learned, what to reopen, what to improve next week.

06 · Conceptual primer

Deal terms without the theatre

Instruments change ownership, control, and downside. Read the actual documents with qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction — this is orientation, not a substitute.

SAFE

A contract for future equity subject to its text — not ordinary shares today, and not automatically “free” or dilution-free. Cap, discount, and conversion mechanics matter.

Convertible note

Debt that may convert. Can include maturity, interest, priority, and other obligations that a SAFE does not automatically create.

Priced round

Shares at a negotiated price — often with fuller governance and investor rights. Model pool treatment, preferences, and future dilution explicitly.

Cap tables track legal holders, options, SAFEs, notes, and fully diluted percentages. Dilution shrinks ownership percentage; enterprise value can still rise if capital creates more value. Ownership ≠ control ≠ exit proceeds.

09 · Teach others

Turn the library into a workshop

A 90-minute session, discussion prompts, and a quiz bank — so teams share vocabulary instead of slogans. Facilitators get structure; learners get practice, not a lecture.

  • Frame a live decision in the first fifteen minutes
  • Run a premortem and assign a red-team voice
  • Close with a journal entry and a scheduled review
90 min workshop

First 30 minutes

Start with one real decision

  1. 0–5 min — Frame owner, deadline, objective, constraints, do-nothing, door type
  2. 5–10 — Three assumptions, base/upside/downside, what would change your mind
  3. 10–15 — Inversion, premortem, second-order effects
  4. 15–20 — Research, voices, authority, review trigger
  5. 20–25 — Journal the decision with rough probabilities
  6. 25–30 — Schedule review and leading indicators
