# Mental models for decisions

**Purpose:** Use a small toolkit to see choices from multiple angles without pretending a model is reality.

**Who this is for:** Decision-makers who need a practical reasoning checklist.

## Core principles
- **Expected value:** probability × consequence across scenarios; show ranges and assumptions.
- **Inversion:** ask how to cause failure, then remove the most avoidable causes.
- **Second-order thinking:** trace what happens after the first response, including incentives.
- **OODA:** observe, orient, decide, act; shorten the learning loop when conditions move quickly.
- **Regret minimisation:** consider which choice your future self can explain, including the cost of inaction.
- **Margin of safety:** leave room for forecast error, cash shocks, and execution slippage.

## How decisions actually get made
Use no more than three models in a meeting. First frame the objective; then apply the models to different failure modes. Make assumptions visible. A model that cannot change the decision is decoration, not analysis.

## Worked mini-example
A company considers adding a second sales channel. EV compares incremental gross profit, implementation cost, and downside if the channel cannibalises existing sales. Inversion asks how channel conflict could destroy partner trust. OODA suggests a regional pilot with weekly observation rather than a national launch.

## Common mistakes
- Stacking frameworks until nobody knows the decision.
- Treating a probability as objective truth.
- Ignoring incentives created by a metric or compensation plan.
- Using “regret” to rationalise fear rather than compare paths.

## Practice exercise (12 minutes)
For one live decision, write one paragraph each for EV, inversion, second-order effects, and a fast feedback loop. Circle the one insight that would actually change your plan.
