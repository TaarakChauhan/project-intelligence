# One-way vs two-way doors

**Purpose:** Match decision speed and ceremony to reversibility and downside.

**Who this is for:** Founders, operators, investors, and teams overloaded by approvals.

## Core principles
1. A **two-way door** is reversible at tolerable cost; bias toward speed and a bounded experiment.
2. A **one-way door** is hard to reverse or carries asymmetric harm; invest in evidence, dissent, and explicit sign-off.
3. Reversibility is contextual: a product toggle may be reversible technically but not reputationally.
4. Define the exit or rollback before committing.

## How decisions actually get made
Ask: “What is the cost and time to undo this? Who bears the downside? Can we stage it?” For two-way doors, appoint one owner, set a deadline, and run a small test. For one-way doors, use a written memo, independent challenge, legal/financial review where relevant, and a board or IC decision when authority requires it.

## Worked mini-example
Changing a landing-page headline is a two-way door: ship to a small audience, measure conversion, and roll back. Signing an exclusive five-year distribution agreement is a one-way door: model obligations, alternatives, termination rights, concentration risk, and approval authority first.

## Common mistakes
- Treating every choice as a crisis and creating approval bottlenecks.
- Calling a decision reversible when customer trust or regulatory exposure cannot be restored.
- Failing to set a rollback trigger.
- Confusing consensus with accountability.

## Practice exercise (8 minutes)
List five decisions on your current plate. Label each one-way, two-way, or mixed. For each mixed decision, design a smaller reversible step and name its rollback trigger.
