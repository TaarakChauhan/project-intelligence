# Decision quality vs outcome

**Purpose:** Learn to judge a decision by the process and information available at the time, not by luck.

**Who this is for:** Anyone making consequential business or investment choices.

## Core principles
1. Separate **decision quality** (framing, evidence, alternatives, probabilities, process) from **outcome quality** (what happened).
2. A good process can lose because the world is uncertain; a weak process can win by chance.
3. Score the forecast and the process separately. Ask, “What did we know then?”
4. Improve the controllable loop: better assumptions, disconfirming evidence, and a planned review.

## How it works in practice
Write a short decision record before acting. Include the base case, key risks, confidence, and what would change your mind. At review, compare the record with reality without rewriting history. Treat an outcome as evidence about assumptions, not as a moral verdict on the decision-maker.

## Worked mini-example
A retailer tests a new location. The team documents 60% confidence of break-even within 12 months, notes construction risk, and sets a six-month kill trigger. A road closure causes a loss. The outcome is poor, but the process may still be sound. If the team ignored the closure risk and never set a trigger, the process needs work even if the store had happened to succeed.

## Common mistakes
- Calling every win “skill” and every loss “bad judgment.”
- Changing the original forecast after seeing the result.
- Using “uncertainty” to avoid stating assumptions.
- Reviewing only financial results and not the quality of the information-gathering process.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
Choose one recent decision. Write the decision as it was known then, three alternatives including do nothing, your probability for each major outcome, and two pieces of information you wish you had. Grade process from 1–5 and outcome separately.
