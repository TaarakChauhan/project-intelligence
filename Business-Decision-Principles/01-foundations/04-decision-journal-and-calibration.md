# Decision journal and calibration

**Purpose:** Turn decisions into a dataset for improving judgment and forecasting.

**Who this is for:** Investors, founders, operators, and boards that want learning rather than anecdotes.

## Core principles
1. Record the choice, date, owner, alternatives, assumptions, probabilities, and evidence before action.
2. Separate what is forecastable from what is unknowable.
3. Revisit at a planned date; do not wait only for spectacular success or failure.
4. Calibration means that events assigned 70% should occur roughly 70% of the time across a comparable set—not that every individual forecast must be right.

## How decisions actually get made
Keep the journal short enough to use. Tag decisions by type, confidence, and horizon. At review, compare predicted ranges with actuals, identify base-rate misses, and adjust the process. A group can use anonymous initial forecasts before discussion to reduce anchoring.

## Worked mini-example
An angel records a 25% chance of a 5× return, 50% chance of a 1×–2× return, and 25% chance of loss, based on retention evidence and a crowded market. At 18 months, the company is alive but flat. The review asks whether the probabilities were reasonable given the evidence, not whether “alive” proves the thesis.

## Common mistakes
- Journalling only large decisions.
- Recording certainty after the fact.
- Confusing precision (e.g., 63%) with accuracy.
- Failing to define what “success” means and when it will be measured.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
Create three probability forecasts for the next 90 days: one personal operating metric, one company metric, and one external event. Write what would make you update each forecast.
