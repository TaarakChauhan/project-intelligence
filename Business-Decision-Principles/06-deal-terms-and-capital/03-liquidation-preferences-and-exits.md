# Liquidation preferences and exits

**Purpose:** Understand why exit proceeds depend on preferences, participation, seniority, and the waterfall.

**Who this is for:** Founders, angels, VCs, and board members.

## Core principles
- A liquidation preference gives a class priority to proceeds before common holders, as defined by the documents.
- Key variables include multiple, participating/non-participating treatment, seniority, conversion rights, dividends, and carve-outs.
- Model the waterfall at low, middle, and high exit values; headline ownership alone misleads.
- Preferences can affect incentives, future financing, and who bears downside.

## How decisions actually get made
Read the charter and financing agreements, not a summary slide. Build a simple waterfall and ask counsel to verify interpretation. Compare the founder/investor outcome under conversion and preference elections.

## Worked mini-example
An investor owns 20% common-equivalent but has a 1× non-participating preference. At a low exit, taking the preference may be better; at a high exit, converting to 20% may be better. A participating preference would have a different waterfall. Exact documents control.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming 20% ownership means 20% of every exit.
- Ignoring senior liquidation preferences.
- Treating “standard” as universal.
- Negotiating economics without considering future rounds.

## Practice exercise (12 minutes)
Draw a three-step waterfall for an exit below, near, and above invested capital. Label each unknown term that requires counsel or the actual charter.
