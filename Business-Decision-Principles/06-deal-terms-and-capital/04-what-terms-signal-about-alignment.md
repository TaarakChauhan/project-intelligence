# What terms signal about alignment

**Purpose:** Read financing terms as incentives and future relationship design, not just price.

**Who this is for:** Founders, investors, and board members negotiating capital.

## Core principles
- Terms allocate economics, control, information, downside, and future choices.
- A term can be reasonable in isolation but harmful in combination.
- Alignment includes behaviour: transparency, decision rights, follow-on expectations, and how parties handle bad news.
- Ask what future scenario each term is designed to protect.

## How decisions actually get made
Translate every term into “who benefits, when, and at whose cost?” Model an up round, flat round, down round, and low exit. Discuss board composition, protective provisions, pro-rata, information, and founder vesting with advisers.

## Worked mini-example
An investor offers a higher valuation but requests aggressive control and a large preference. Another offers lower valuation with transparent governance and useful reserves. The better decision depends on strategy, risk, and relationship—not valuation alone.

## Common mistakes
- Optimising headline valuation.
- Treating governance rights as free.
- Failing to test a term in a downside scenario.
- Assuming conflict is impossible because the investor is friendly today.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
For a hypothetical term sheet, list five terms and write the scenario each protects, the trade-off, and the alignment question to ask.
