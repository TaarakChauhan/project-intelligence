# SAFEs, notes, and priced rounds

**Purpose:** Compare common early financing instruments at a conceptual level.

**Who this is for:** Founders, angels, and operators preparing for a financing conversation.

## Core principles
- A **SAFE** is a contract for future equity subject to its text; it is not ordinary shares today.
- A **convertible note** is debt that may convert, with maturity, interest, and other terms that matter.
- A **priced round** issues shares at a negotiated price and typically brings fuller governance and investor rights.
- Cap, discount, valuation method, pro-rata, information rights, seniority, and side letters can materially change outcomes.

## How decisions actually get made
Model the instrument against several next-round valuations and financing sizes. Read the exact form and amendments. Explain how current and future holders are affected. Use securities counsel in the relevant jurisdiction; “standard” does not mean consequence-free.

## Worked mini-example
Two SAFEs have the same cash amount but different valuation caps and pro-rata rights. In a modest next round, the lower cap may convert into materially more ownership; in a large round, the discount may govern. Only a cap-table model and documents answer the question.

## Common mistakes
- Calling a SAFE debt or “free money.”
- Discussing valuation without conversion mechanics.
- Ignoring MFN, pro-rata, or side letters.
- Signing before understanding authority and securities compliance.

## Practice exercise (12 minutes)
List each economic and governance term in a hypothetical instrument. Write the question a founder and investor should each ask about cap, discount, timing, rights, and downside.
