# Team, market, product: jockey versus horse

**Purpose:** Evaluate founder capability, market structure, and product evidence without reducing judgment to a slogan.

**Who this is for:** Early-stage investors and founders explaining their edge.

## Core principles
1. Team and market interact: a strong team in a tiny market may not fit a venture mandate; a large market with weak execution is not enough.
2. Look for specific founder-market fit: insight, access, credibility, speed of learning, and resilience.
3. Product evidence should include behaviour—activation, retention, usage, willingness to pay—not only demos.
4. Ask what would need to be true for the team to win against better-funded competitors.

## How decisions actually get made
Triangulate founder claims with customer behaviour, references, decision speed, and how the team handles bad news. Examine market timing, substitutes, budgets, regulation, and distribution. Avoid treating charisma, pedigree, or a famous employer as a proxy for execution.

## Worked mini-example
Two founders have similar demos. Team A has domain insight and five retained design partners; Team B has a slicker demo but no evidence users return. Team A may have stronger early evidence even if the product is less polished.

## Common mistakes
- “Jockey versus horse” as an excuse to ignore the market.
- Overweighting founder confidence or presentation quality.
- Calling a market large without a credible wedge and buyer.
- Using only friendly references.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
Write five observable behaviours that would support founder-market fit. For a company you know, mark each as evidenced, asserted, or unknown.
