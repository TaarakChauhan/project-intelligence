# Unit economics and capital efficiency

**Purpose:** Use economics to understand what growth costs, what it earns, and what assumptions make the model fragile.

**Who this is for:** VCs, founders, and owners managing growth.

## Core principles
- Define revenue, gross margin, contribution margin, CAC, payback, retention, expansion, and burn consistently.
- Analyse cohorts; blended averages can hide deterioration.
- LTV is a scenario estimate. Churn, gross margin, contract length, expansion, and discounting drive it.
- Burn multiple (net cash burn ÷ net new recurring revenue, if that is the chosen definition) is useful only with a stated period and denominator.
- Capital efficiency is not “spend as little as possible”; it is buying learning and durable growth at a risk-appropriate cost.

## How decisions actually get made
Reconcile metrics to cash and accounting records. Separate one-time contracts, pilots, founder-led sales, and channel subsidies. Model downside if acquisition costs rise, churn worsens, or a hiring plan slips. Compare a growth plan with a slower plan and the financing each requires.

## Worked mini-example
A SaaS company reports attractive LTV/CAC, but the calculation uses logo churn rather than revenue churn and ignores support labour. Rebuilding by cohort lowers the estimate and lengthens payback. The decision may still be to grow, but with a more honest cash plan.

## Common mistakes
- Treating LTV/CAC as a universal threshold.
- Mixing bookings, billings, revenue, and cash.
- Ignoring working capital and implementation costs.
- Optimising burn multiple by starving the experiments that reveal PMF.

## Practice exercise (15 minutes)
Take a hypothetical month: revenue $100, gross margin 70%, sales and marketing $60, support $15, and net new recurring revenue $40. Define your metric choices, calculate contribution before overhead, and list two missing inputs before calling the model efficient.
