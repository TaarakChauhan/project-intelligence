# How VCs really decide

**Purpose:** Understand venture decisions as portfolio underwriting under uncertainty, not a product beauty contest.

**Who this is for:** Aspiring VCs, founders fundraising, and angels comparing processes.

## Core principles
1. VC returns are distributed unevenly; a fund must underwrite both company quality and the chance of a fund-relevant outcome.
2. The question is not only “Is this good?” but “Why this company, why now, why this fund, and what evidence supports the path?”
3. Deal selection is a core value-creation activity; access and support matter, but they do not repair a weak thesis.
4. Portfolio construction changes the acceptable risk, ownership, reserve, and stage profile.

## Research anchor
Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan, and Strebulaev surveyed **885 VCs at 681 firms**. Approximately **95%** mentioned team as important and approximately **47%** called it the most important factor; business model was mentioned by approximately **83%**, product by **74%**, and market by **68%**. Deal selection ranked most important among sourcing, selection, and value-add activities. These are survey findings, not a universal scorecard.

## How decisions actually get made
A typical process moves from thesis-based screen to partner-sponsored deep dive, references and specialist diligence, an investment memo, partner discussion, and an IC decision. Different firms compress or reorder this. Internal incentives, fund reserves, ownership targets, conflicts, and partner conviction all affect the final call.

## Worked mini-example
A seed company has strong early retention but a small initial niche. One fund passes because it cannot see a fund-sized path; another invests because the niche is an entry wedge and the team has a credible expansion plan. Both may be rational given different portfolio mandates.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the loudest partner’s conviction as evidence.
- Confusing a fund’s brand with its ability to help this company.
- Ignoring ownership, reserves, and follow-on capacity.
- Using survey percentages as a mechanical ranking model.

## Practice exercise (12 minutes)
Write a one-page “why this fund / why this company / why now” thesis. Add the portfolio constraint that could make an otherwise good company a pass.
