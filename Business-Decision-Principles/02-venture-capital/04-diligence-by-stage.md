# Diligence by stage

**Purpose:** Change the evidence standard as a company moves from possibility to repeatability.

**Who this is for:** VCs and founders deciding what “ready” means.

## Core principles
- **Pre-seed:** vision, founder insight, problem intensity, early tests, technical feasibility, and learning velocity.
- **Seed:** evidence of demand, early retention or usage, customer discovery, initial distribution, and a credible next milestone.
- **Series A:** repeatable acquisition and retention signals, coherent unit economics direction, team scaling, and a model for the next phase.
- **Growth:** durable cohorts, efficient growth, forecast reliability, governance, path to profitability or financing independence, and market structure.

## How decisions actually get made
Do not demand late-stage proof early, but do demand stage-appropriate proof. Ask “what risk is this round meant to retire?” A seed round may buy product-market-fit learning; a Series A may buy repeatable growth. Milestones should be observable, not vague.

## Worked mini-example
At pre-seed, a founder has 20 design partners and a credible technical plan; no one expects mature LTV/CAC. At Series A, the same story without cohort retention or a repeatable sales motion is a serious gap.

## Common mistakes
- Applying growth-stage metrics to an idea-stage company.
- Excusing missing basic hygiene because the company is early.
- Confusing a large pilot with repeatable demand.
- Treating a financing round as proof that prior risk disappeared.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
For each stage, write one “must know,” one “nice to know,” and one question that would be premature. Then define the milestone the next round should purchase.
