# Investment memo anatomy

**Purpose:** Write an IC memo that makes the decision, uncertainty, and downside legible.

**Who this is for:** Investors, founders reviewing a fund's process, and IC members.

## Core principles
1. Lead with the decision requested: amount, instrument, ownership target, reserves, and conditions.
2. Distinguish verified facts, management claims, analyst assumptions, and open questions.
3. State the thesis in one paragraph and the three ways it can be wrong.
4. Include market, product, team, traction, economics, competition, legal/ESG, terms, scenarios, and portfolio fit.
5. Recommend approve, pass, or defer—not “interesting.”

## How decisions actually get made
A useful memo is short enough to read and detailed enough to audit. Link exhibits to primary evidence. Show base/upside/downside cases, sensitivity to key assumptions, and what the investment must achieve to matter to the fund. Record dissent and conflicts.

## Worked mini-example
Instead of “large market and strong team,” write: “Approve $1.5M seed subject to IP assignment confirmation; thesis depends on 30-day retention in the target workflow and two independent paid references; downside is customer concentration; reserve $X only if milestone Y is met.”

## Common mistakes
- Hiding risks in an appendix.
- Using false precision in valuation scenarios.
- Omitting why the fund is the right owner.
- Treating terms as a footnote.
