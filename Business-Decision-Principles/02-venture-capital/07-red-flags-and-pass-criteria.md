# Red flags and pass criteria

**Purpose:** Turn discomfort into testable evidence and clear pass rules.

**Who this is for:** Investment teams and founders preparing for hard questions.

## Core principles
- Red flags are prompts for investigation, not automatic proof of failure.
- Pre-commit material pass criteria before advocacy pressure rises.
- Look for inconsistent metrics, weak retention, overstated TAM, single-customer dependency, and founder conflict.
- Assess whether a risk is known, bounded, mitigated, or simply hand-waved.

## How decisions actually get made
Create a red-flag register with severity, evidence, owner, and decision impact. Escalate integrity or undisclosed-conflict issues immediately. A pass can be the right decision even when the company is good if evidence does not fit the mandate, price, ownership, or portfolio.

## Worked mini-example
Revenue grew 80%, but a reconciliation shows a single customer contributed most of the increase and is month-to-month. The corrected base case exposes concentration and retention risk. The team can pass, wait for renewal evidence, or invest only if terms and mandate make the risk acceptable.

## Common mistakes
- Treating every anomaly as fraud.
- Letting a charismatic founder explain away contradictory data.
- Defining pass criteria after hearing the pitch.
- Keeping a deal alive because “we might regret missing it.”

## Practice exercise (8 minutes)
Draft five pass criteria for a seed investment. For each, identify a minimum evidence threshold and a reason to defer rather than reject.
