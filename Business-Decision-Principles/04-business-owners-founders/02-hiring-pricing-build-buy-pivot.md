# Hiring, pricing, build, buy, pivot

**Purpose:** Apply one decision process to common owner choices without hiding trade-offs.

**Who this is for:** Owners and founders making resource-allocation decisions.

## Core principles
- **Hiring:** define the bottleneck, outcomes, ramp time, manager capacity, and reversible contractor/test options.
- **Pricing:** test willingness to pay, value delivered, segmentation, and implementation costs; communicate changes clearly.
- **Build vs buy:** compare total cost, speed, strategic differentiation, lock-in, security, and maintenance.
- **Pivot:** identify which assumption failed, what evidence supports the new direction, and what assets transfer.

## How decisions actually get made
Write a base case and downside case. Separate a learning experiment from a full commitment. Use customer interviews and behavioural data, not internal preference alone. Decide what would cause you to stop, narrow, or double down.

## Worked mini-example
A service business considers hiring a salesperson. Before committing to salary, it tests a commission-only channel or founder-led scripts, verifies lead-to-close economics, and sets a 90-day evidence review. The experiment informs hiring without pretending it proves a long-term plan.

## Common mistakes
- Hiring to relieve discomfort rather than a measurable constraint.
- Pricing from competitor menus without knowing value.
- Ignoring integration and switching costs in “buy.”
- Calling any new feature a pivot.

## Practice exercise (12 minutes)
Pick build, buy, hire, or pivot. Score options on customer value, speed, cash, reversibility, capability, and downside. List the smallest test that can remove one uncertainty.
