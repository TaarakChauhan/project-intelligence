# Disagree and commit: decision rights

**Purpose:** Create productive dissent without leaving decisions permanently open.

**Who this is for:** Leadership teams, owners, and boards.

## Core principles
1. Invite dissent before the decision; do not punish a well-reasoned challenge.
2. One person owns the call; contributors do not need unanimity.
3. “Disagree and commit” requires a clear decision, rationale, boundaries, and review trigger.
4. Escalate decisions based on authority and risk, not volume or politics.

## How decisions actually get made
Use a RACI-like map: recommend, input, decide, execute, review. Ask dissenters to identify the assumption and propose a test. Once decided, document what commitment means and what evidence would reopen the choice.

## Worked mini-example
The product lead recommends delaying a feature; sales disagrees due to a customer promise. The CEO decides a limited contractual workaround, assigns owners, and sets a 30-day customer and engineering review. The dissent becomes a test, not a veto.

## Common mistakes
- Using the phrase to silence dissent.
- Reopening every decision without new evidence.
- Letting a senior person decide outside their authority.
- Failing to communicate the decision to executors.

## Practice exercise (8 minutes)
For one recurring meeting, write who recommends, who decides, who must be consulted, and the trigger for escalation.
