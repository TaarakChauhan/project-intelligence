# Cash runway and survival decisions

**Purpose:** Make cash risk concrete early enough to preserve choices.

**Who this is for:** Owners, founders, and finance leaders.

## Core principles
- Runway is cash available divided by defined monthly net burn; it is a scenario, not a promise.
- Cash timing, working capital, debt service, taxes, and commitments matter as much as P&L.
- Protect the core engine first; make cuts that preserve learning and customer trust where possible.
- Set trigger points before panic: hiring pause, vendor renegotiation, pricing action, financing outreach, or wind-down advice.

## How decisions actually get made
Maintain a rolling cash forecast with base, downside, and severe cases. Reconcile weekly when risk is high. Convert each trigger into a pre-authorised action and owner. Seek qualified accounting, insolvency, tax, and legal advice when obligations may not be met.

## Worked mini-example
A business shows 10 months of runway at current burn but only five months after a quarterly tax payment and seasonal receivables delay. The corrected plan starts financing outreach now and pauses non-essential hiring.

## Common mistakes
- Using last month’s burn during a changing growth plan.
- Counting receivables as cash without timing.
- Cutting customer support blindly.
- Treating a financing hope as cash on hand.

## Practice exercise (12 minutes)
List opening cash, committed outflows, expected collections, debt/tax dates, and three burn scenarios for the next six months. Circle the first trigger that changes behaviour.
