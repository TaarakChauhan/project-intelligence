# Raising capital as a decision

**Purpose:** Treat fundraising as a strategic choice with price, control, obligations, and timing—not merely a cash target.

**Who this is for:** Founders and owners considering debt, equity, grants, or bootstrapping.

## Core principles
- Raise for a specific milestone and financing purpose, not because a headline round feels validating.
- Compare dilution, control, repayment, covenants, reporting, investor fit, speed, and downside.
- Timing matters: raise before the runway cliff, but avoid selling too much uncertainty if a milestone is near.
- Model the “do not raise” plan and what it forces you to stop, delay, or learn.

## How decisions actually get made
Build a 12–24 month cash plan with scenarios. Identify the milestone the capital buys and the evidence that unlocks the next option. Compare instruments with professional advice. Keep the cap table and use-of-proceeds narrative honest.

## Worked mini-example
A company can raise $1M now at a difficult valuation or reach a paid-retention milestone in six months using a narrower plan. The choice depends on survival risk, dilution, milestone probability, and strategic need—not valuation pride alone.

## Common mistakes
- Raising the maximum because it is available.
- Ignoring investor time horizon and board rights.
- Treating a SAFE as “not dilution.”
- Waiting until cash is nearly exhausted.

## Practice exercise (15 minutes)
Write a use-of-proceeds table: amount, milestone, owner, date, and evidence. Add the cost of the financing and the failure mode if the milestone slips.
