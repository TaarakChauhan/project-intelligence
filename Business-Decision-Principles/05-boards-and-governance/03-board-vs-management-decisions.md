# Board vs management decisions

**Purpose:** Clarify oversight, delegation, and escalation so governance does not become operational gridlock.

**Who this is for:** Directors, CEOs, founders, and investors with board rights.

## Core principles
- The board governs: purpose, CEO oversight, strategy/risk oversight, major capital and structural decisions, and accountability.
- Management executes within delegated authority and reports honestly.
- Boundaries should be explicit: reserved matters, budgets, signing authority, hiring authority, and escalation triggers.
- Boards should challenge outcomes and assumptions without becoming a shadow management team.

## How decisions actually get made
Create a decision-rights schedule. Board materials should surface exceptions, trends, risks, and choices—not only status updates. The chair protects agenda time and the CEO owns execution. Revisit delegation as the company grows.

## Worked mini-example
Management can hire within an approved budget. The board approves a CEO appointment, acquisition, or financing above a defined threshold. A near-threshold event is escalated early rather than engineered around the rule.

## Common mistakes
- Investors using information rights as operating authority.
- Boards approving details while missing strategic risk.
- CEOs presenting surprises late.
- Vague reserved matters that invite conflict.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
Draft a one-page decision-rights table for hiring, pricing, debt, equity, acquisitions, and product risk. Mark who recommends, decides, and reviews.
