# Conflicted transactions

**Purpose:** Recognise conflicts early and use a process that protects the corporation and its stakeholders.

**Who this is for:** Directors, founders, investors, and board observers.

## Core principles
1. A conflict can be financial, relational, role-based, or informational; disclose it early.
2. Separate the interested person from the independent decision process where required or prudent.
3. Test fairness with independent benchmarks, alternatives, expert advice, and negotiation.
4. Follow statute, articles, shareholder agreements, policies, and minutes requirements.

## How decisions actually get made
At agenda intake, flag related parties. Clarify who may receive information, participate, vote, or abstain. Obtain independent advice when material. Record disclosure, process, alternatives, and rationale. Never backfill a process after the transaction has closed.

## Worked mini-example
A company proposes buying software from a director’s other business. The director discloses the interest and leaves negotiation to independent directors. The board compares alternatives and documents the price and operational rationale.

## Common mistakes
- “Everyone knew” instead of formal disclosure.
- Letting the conflicted person control the information flow.
- Assuming a good price eliminates process risk.
- Forgetting minority shareholder, tax, privacy, or regulatory implications.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
Map a hypothetical related-party transaction: conflict, disclosure, independent participants, evidence, approvals, and minute language. List what counsel must confirm.
