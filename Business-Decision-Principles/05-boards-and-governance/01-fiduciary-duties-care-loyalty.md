# Fiduciary duties: care and loyalty

**Purpose:** Provide a plain-language governance overview while respecting jurisdictional limits.

**Who this is for:** Directors, founders, investors, and governance learners.

## Core principles
- Directors generally act for the corporation (and in some contexts its stockholders/shareholders), not a nominating investor alone; exact duties depend on jurisdiction and entity.
- **Care:** be informed, attentive, and appropriately supervised.
- **Loyalty:** act in good faith for the corporation, manage conflicts, and avoid using corporate opportunities improperly.
- Good governance is a process: information, deliberation, records, independence, and follow-through.

## How decisions actually get made
Boards set agendas around strategy, risk, capital, leadership, and compliance. Management supplies timely information; directors ask questions and document material deliberation. Canadian and U.S. rules differ, and corporate documents and case law matter. Obtain local counsel for an actual issue.

## Worked mini-example
A director is asked to approve a supplier owned by a family member. The board identifies the conflict, discloses it, follows the applicable abstention/approval process, obtains independent benchmarks, and records why the transaction is or is not in the corporation’s interest.

## Common mistakes
- Treating a board seat as a personal agent role.
- Assuming good intentions cure a conflict.
- Rubber-stamping management information.
- Applying Delaware concepts automatically to a Canadian corporation.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
For a hypothetical board decision, list information needed, questions to ask, interests that may conflict, and the record the minutes should capture.
