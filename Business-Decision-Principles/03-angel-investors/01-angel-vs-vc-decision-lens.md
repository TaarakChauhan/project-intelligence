# Angel vs VC: the decision lens

**Purpose:** Understand what changes when an individual invests personal capital rather than a fund.

**Who this is for:** New and experienced angels, founders, and family-business investors.

## Core principles
- Angels have personal liquidity, concentration, tax, time, and emotional constraints; a fund has mandate, reserves, and fiduciary obligations to its investors.
- An angel can invest for learning, community, strategic fit, or financial return—but must name the objective.
- Helpful access is not the same as a promise of value-add.
- Portfolio thinking reduces the temptation to make one deal carry a life-changing outcome.

## How decisions actually get made
Set a written cheque range, stage/sector boundary, annual loss budget, follow-on policy, and time commitment. Use a lighter but real diligence process. Decide whether you can support the company without becoming an informal operator.

## Worked mini-example
An angel likes a local founder and can open two doors, but the company is outside their risk budget and creates concentration. The disciplined answer is pass, or a smaller amount only if it fits the written portfolio plan—not an exception justified by enthusiasm.

## Common mistakes
- Copying VC ownership targets without a fund structure.
- Investing money needed for near-term living or debt obligations.
- Confusing friendship with diligence.
- Promising introductions without capacity.

## Practice exercise (8 minutes)
Write your personal mandate: cheque range, maximum portfolio concentration, time budget, stage, excluded risks, and what “success” means.
