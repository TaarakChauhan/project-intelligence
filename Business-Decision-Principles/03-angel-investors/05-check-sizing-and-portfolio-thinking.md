# Check sizing and portfolio thinking

**Purpose:** Size an angel investment so one outcome does not dominate your financial plan.

**Who this is for:** Angels deciding initial and follow-on cheques.

## Core principles
1. Start with an amount you can lose without changing essential life plans.
2. Reserve follow-on capital only if your strategy and access justify it; do not assume you will always get allocation.
3. Consider ownership, dilution, time to liquidity, and the probability of a total loss.
4. Diversification is not a guarantee; early-stage outcomes are highly uncertain and illiquid.

## How decisions actually get made
Set a portfolio budget and maximum exposure before reviewing a deal. Model a small set of outcomes, fees/taxes where relevant, and future rounds. Terms such as SAFE cap, discount, and pro-rata rights can change exposure; read the actual documents.

## Worked mini-example
An angel has $40,000 available for a year and wants four initial bets plus a reserve. A $25,000 exception would crowd out the plan and create emotional pressure. A $7,500 cheque may fit, or the correct choice may be to pass.

## Common mistakes
- Using a famous investor’s cheque size as a benchmark.
- Treating pro-rata as guaranteed liquidity or allocation.
- Ignoring currency, tax, and concentration.
- Averaging down because of sunk cost.

## Practice exercise (10 minutes)
Write three portfolio scenarios (conservative, base, aggressive). Allocate initial and reserve amounts, then identify which assumption would make the plan unsafe.
