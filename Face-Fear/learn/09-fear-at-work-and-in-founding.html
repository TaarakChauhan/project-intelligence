Fear at work and in founding

Fear of failure in founders, psychological safety in teams, and leading people who are afraid.

Work is full of fear, even when nobody says so. Fear of looking incompetent, of missing targets, of the next round of layoffs, of a launch that nobody notices. For founders and leaders, fear is part of the job. The question is whether it runs the job.

Fear of failure in founders

Research on entrepreneurs, including a review by Cacciotti and Hayton, shows that fear of failure is not simply a brake. For some people it stops them from starting or makes them freeze. For others it drives harder preparation, faster learning, and more careful planning.

What seems to matter is how the fear is held. When failure feels like a judgment on who you are, fear tends to block. When failure feels like information about what to try next, fear tends to fuel action.

Psychological safety

Amy Edmondson's research on work teams found that teams learn more when people believe it is safe to speak up, ask questions, and admit mistakes. She called this psychological safety. It does not mean comfort or low standards. It means people are not afraid of being punished or embarrassed for honesty.

Fearful teams hide problems. Problems that stay hidden get bigger. That is why fear is costly for any organisation.

Leading people who are afraid

  • Say the uncertainty out loud. People fear what is unspoken more than what is named.
  • Share what you know, what you do not know, and when you will know more.
  • Respond to bad news with curiosity first. Thank people for raising it.
  • Admit your own mistakes. It gives others permission to admit theirs.
  • Separate high standards from fear. You can expect excellent work without threatening people.

Your own fear as a leader

Leaders feel fear too, and pretending otherwise usually leaks out as control, irritability, or indecision. It helps to have a place to process fear, such as a peer, mentor, or coach, so it does not land on the team.

Courage at work often looks small. Sending the honest update. Making the call you have been avoiding. Asking for help. The Founder Path section has a lesson on acting with courage when the load is heavy.

Key ideas

  • Fear of failure can block founders or push them to prepare and learn. How it is held matters.
  • Psychological safety, the belief that honesty will not be punished, helps teams learn.
  • Leaders reduce fear by naming uncertainty and responding to bad news with curiosity.
  • A leader's unspoken fear often shows up as control or indecision.

Try this: The fear in the room

  1. Think of your team, or a group you belong to.
  2. Write one thing people seem afraid to say out loud.
  3. Write what that silence costs the group.
  4. Choose one action that would make it a little safer to say, such as asking a direct question or sharing your own mistake first.
