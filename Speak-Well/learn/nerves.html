Lesson 04

Nerves & confidence

~8 min read · Confidence

Stage fright is common and trainable. Joe Kowan’s TED talk, How I beat stage fright, is a useful, human starting point: name the fear, build coping systems, and keep showing up.

What nerves usually mean

Your body is preparing for a high-stakes social moment. Heart rate up, dry mouth, racing thoughts — these are signals, not proof you will fail. The goal is not zero nerves. The goal is usable energy.

A simple pre-talk routine

  1. Breathing: 4-7-8 × 3 (inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8)
  2. Open stance for 60 seconds
  3. Say your first line out loud five times with energy
  4. Walk on with the intention to help one person in the room

About “power posing”

Amy Cuddy’s talk Your body language may shape who you are popularized power posing. TED and later research note that the scientific claims are contested. You can still use open posture as a practical comfort habit — just do not treat it as settled biology.

Exposure beats pep talks

Weekly low-stakes reps (Toastmasters Table Topics, filmed 60-second talks, standups) shrink fear faster than motivational quotes.
