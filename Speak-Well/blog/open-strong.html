How to open a talk in the first 15 seconds

Speak Well Blog

Audiences decide quickly whether to lean in. Your opening is not a throat-clearing ritual. It is the first proof that you will be worth their time.

Three openings to practice

  1. Story: a 20-second moment that creates a human stake.
  2. Question: one question the audience can answer silently.
  3. Startling fact: a concrete number or contrast that reframes the topic.

Avoid: “Today I’m going to talk about…”, long bios, and apologizing for being nervous.

Write three openings for the same talk. Film all three. Keep the one that makes you want to keep listening.

Drill

Take tomorrow’s standup or class update. Rewrite the first sentence three ways. Deliver each once.
