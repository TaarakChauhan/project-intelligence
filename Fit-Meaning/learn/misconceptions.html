Lesson 3

Common mistakes

Educational lesson · Fit Meaning

Once you have the mechanism, most public uses of the phrase fail in predictable ways. Learn to hear them in ordinary talk — including the political one.

Evolution is not a ladder

School posters still show a climb from ape to spear-carrying man. The picture teaches the wrong idea: that evolution heads toward us, and that "more evolved" means "more like a human."

Natural selection has no preferred direction toward complexity, intelligence, or us. It favors what reproduces in that environment. A tapeworm is not a failed human. A bacterium is not waiting to be promoted. Lineages split, specialize, lose traits, and go extinct. Humans are one twig, not the point of the tree.

"Higher" and "lower" are leftover language from older natural history. In selection terms they do no work. A moss can be well matched to its patch of shade. That is not a lower achievement; it is a different environment.

Related trap: treating living chimpanzees as "our ancestors," or "missing links" as unfinished people. Living chimpanzees are cousins, not rehearsal versions of us. Shared ancestors are in the past.

"Nature red in tooth and claw"

The line is from Tennyson, not Darwin, and it stuck because it is vivid. It suggests that the real process is constant slaughter, and that kindness is a human overlay.

Competition is real. Predation is real. Scarcity is real. But cooperation can be selected too, whenever heritable social behavior raises the reproductive success of those who carry it — through shared defense, shared food, care of young, or benefits to relatives who share genes. Social insects, warning calls, and human alliances are not exceptions that sit outside biology. They are biology.

Two errors travel together here. First, treating every organism as a lone gladiator. Second, treating "selfish genes" talk as if it meant selfish organisms. A gene that promotes helping kin can spread because copies of it sit in those kin. The accounting is still reproductive success. You do not need to flatten life into a bar fight to keep the theory.

Selection can also favor restraint: a display instead of a fight, a territory instead of a massacre. Violence is one possible path to leaving descendants. It is not the definition of fitness.

"If it is natural, it is good"

This is the naturalistic fallacy in everyday clothes: sliding from this happens in nature to this is right, or we should do it.

Smallpox is natural. Infanticide occurs in some species. So does pair bonding. So does abandoning young. Nature is not a moral manual. Natural selection explains how traits persist; it does not certify them. Medicine, law, and kindness are not "against evolution." They are things humans do, using brains that themselves evolved, to pursue values we choose.

The reverse error is just as sloppy: calling something "unnatural" as if that settled whether it is bad. Eyeglasses, agriculture, and antibiotics are "unnatural" in a narrow sense; that does not make them illegitimate.

When someone says "it's survival of the fittest" to end an argument about policy, they have usually changed the subject from biology to ethics without admitting it.

Social Darwinism (brief and honest)

In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, writers and politicians borrowed evolutionary language to justify the social order they already wanted. The cluster of ideas later called social Darwinism treated competition among people, classes, or nations as a law, and treated aid to the poor, regulation, or equality as interference with nature. Spencer's project of applying the phrase beyond biology fed that atmosphere, even when later users went further than he did.

The phrase was also dragged into defenses of empire, racial hierarchy, and later eugenics: attempts to "improve" populations by controlling who reproduces. Those projects claimed the authority of science. They do not follow from evolutionary biology, and biology does not support them as moral or political conclusions.

Why the leap fails:

  • Natural selection is a description of what happens when heritable variation meets differential reproduction. It is not a recommendation.
  • Human worth is not a fitness score. Fitness is not virtue, intelligence, or "quality."
  • Modern societies already change the environment (medicine, food, law). Pretending the existing hierarchy is "what nature selected" ignores history, power, and accident.
  • Genetic variation in humans does not map onto the crude racial categories those movements used, and "more successful in the market" is not "genetically fitter" in the biological sense.

You can reject social Darwinism without pretending selection is fictional. The honest position is: the mechanism is real; the slogan was misused to launder inequality; the misuse was never a scientific result.

Two quieter mistakes

Every trait is an adaptation. No. Some traits are byproducts, leftovers, or the result of genetic drift — random change in how common a gene is, especially in small populations. Drift is not selection. If you cannot name a reproductive advantage, do not invent one.

One "fittest" type wins forever. No. Environments move. Frequency-dependent advantages exist (a rare color may be safer because it is rare). Multiple strategies can persist. The process does not crown a permanent champion.

If you catch yourself saying "the strong survive" as if that were the theory, go back to Lesson 1. If you say "so we shouldn't help," you have left biology. Keep them apart.
