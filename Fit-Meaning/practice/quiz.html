Quiz

Try closed-notes · then reveal the answer key

Ten questions covering the pack. Try them closed-notes. Mix of multiple choice and short answer.

Write your answers first. The answer key is below the ruled line at the bottom. Do not score yourself until you have finished all ten.

1. (multiple choice) Who coined the phrase "survival of the fittest," and how did Darwin use it?

  • A. Darwin coined it in 1859 as the official name of his theory; Spencer later borrowed it for sociology.
  • B. Spencer coined it; Darwin later used it as a synonym for natural selection.
  • C. Wallace coined it as a rejection of Darwin; Darwin refused to use it.
  • D. It was a newspaper headline Darwin never used in his books.

2. (short answer) In one or two sentences, define fitness the way biologists mean it. Then name one ordinary-English meaning that is not the biological one.

3. (multiple choice) A long-lived animal leaves no offspring. A short-lived animal leaves many. In this environment, which is fitter?

  • A. The long-lived animal, because survival is what the phrase names.
  • B. They are equally fit, because both "survived" long enough to be observed.
  • C. The short-lived animal, because fitness is relative reproductive success.
  • D. Neither; fitness only applies to species, not individuals.

4. (short answer) List the three ingredients of natural selection. For each, add a few words saying what goes wrong if it is missing.

5. (multiple choice) Peppered moths in industrial Britain are a textbook case because:

  • A. Moths chose darker wings "in order to" match soot, proving that need causes mutation.
  • B. A heritable color difference changed in frequency as the visual background changed, then reversed when the background lightened.
  • C. Dark moths were stronger fliers, so they outcompeted pale moths in every environment.
  • D. The species climbed a ladder from pale (primitive) to dark (advanced).

6. (short answer) Why is "the bacteria evolved resistance because they needed to survive the antibiotic" the wrong shape of explanation? Rewrite it in selection language.

7. (multiple choice) Which statement about cooperation is most accurate?

  • A. Cooperation is real in humans but cannot evolve, because selection only favors fighting.
  • B. Cooperation never occurs in nature; apparent teamwork is always a trick of the observer.
  • C. Heritable cooperative behavior can spread when it raises the reproductive success of those who carry it (including via benefits to relatives).
  • D. Cooperation disproves natural selection, which is why Darwin ignored social insects.

8. (short answer) Someone says: "Welfare is wrong; it's survival of the fittest." Name two separate mistakes in that move — one about biology, one about ethics or politics.

9. (multiple choice) Genetic drift is:

  • A. Another name for natural selection in small populations.
  • B. Random change in variant frequencies, not caused by a systematic fitness difference.
  • C. What happens when animals physically drift to new islands.
  • D. The process that makes every trait an adaptation.

10. (short answer) Give a present-day example (medicine, conservation, or agriculture) and run it through: what varies, how it is inherited, what the pressure is, who reproduces more. Then state one human-evolution myth this pack asked you to drop.

Reveal answer key

Answer key

Do not read this until you have answered all ten.

1. B. Herbert Spencer coined the phrase in the 1860s; Darwin later adopted it as a synonym for natural selection. A reverses the history. C and D are false.

2. Fitness is relative reproductive success in a given environment (how much a variant contributes to the next generation compared with others). Not the same as: strength, athleticism, health-as-fitness, cruelty, or moral worth. Accept equivalent wording.

3. C. Lifespan is not the score. Reproduction is. D is wrong because selection acts on variation among individuals (or heritable variants), not on a species as a unit. A is the folk slogan.

4. (1) Variation — without differences, nothing to filter. (2) Heredity — without resemblance across generations, the filter does not accumulate. (3) Differential reproduction — without unequal contribution of offspring, frequencies do not change by selection. Wording can vary; all three ideas must be present.

5. B. The established logic is a heritable color polymorphism whose success tracked a changed (then restored) background. A treats mutation as a response to need. C invents a universal athletic advantage. D is the ladder myth.

6. Need does not cause the useful mutation. Resistant variants, if present (from mutation or gene transfer), reproduce more under the drug; sensitive ones do not. The population becomes resistant as a result, not because cells planned it. Accept any rewrite that has variation already in place (or arising undirected), then a filter.

7. C. Cooperation can be selected. A and B treat "red in tooth and claw" as the definition of the process. D is historically and scientifically false.

8. Biology: fitness is not "the strong crush the weak," and selection is not a ranking of human worth; aid changes the environment, it does not "violate a law of nature." Ethics/politics: even if a process occurs in nature, that does not make a policy good (naturalistic fallacy / social Darwinism). The slogan is being used to launder a political conclusion science does not yield.

9. B. Drift is chance, especially in small populations. It is not selection (A), not literal rafting (C), and not a reason to treat every trait as an adaptation (D).

10. Example must include all three ingredients. Acceptable sketches: antibiotic/MRSA resistance; herbicide-resistant weeds; size-selective fishing favoring smaller or earlier-reproducing fish; breeders choosing crop traits (artificial selection counts if labeled). Myth to drop: any of — humans have stopped evolving; civilization is genetically degenerating because the "unfit" reproduce; humans are the top of a ladder; career success equals biological fitness. If the example is missing heredity or a real pressure, mark it incomplete.

Scoring. Nine or ten solid: you can use the idea. Seven or eight: reread the lessons that match the misses (1–3 → Lesson 1; 4–6 → Lesson 2; 7–8 → Lesson 3; 9–10 → Lesson 3–4 and glossary). Below seven: do Session 5 again after a pass through Lessons 1 and 2 with the checklist written out.
