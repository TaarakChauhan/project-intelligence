Instant gratification: why we want it now

Instant gratification is the pull toward a reward right now instead of a bigger one later. A phone check instead of focused work. A purchase today instead of savings next year. This page explains the idea in plain words and collects 62 trusted resources so you can go deeper.

What instant gratification means

Four ideas that explain most of it.

01

Now feels bigger than later

The Cambridge Dictionary calls it pleasure or satisfaction you get right away, without having to wait or work hard. Our brains give a lot of weight to rewards we can have today and shrink rewards that are far away. Economists call this present bias or hyperbolic discounting.

02

The marshmallow test

In the 1960s and 1970s Walter Mischel offered children one treat now or two if they waited. Kids who distracted themselves waited longer. Early follow ups linked waiting to later success, but a larger 2018 study found that link was much weaker once family background was counted.

03

Dopamine is about wanting

Dopamine is often called the pleasure chemical, but research suggests it is more about wanting and expecting a reward. Apps, snacks and shopping can trigger that wanting again and again, which is why quick rewards are so hard to put down.

04

It is not just weak willpower

Waiting only makes sense when you trust the later reward will come. Stress, money worries and broken promises all push people toward now. The good news: small changes to your surroundings often work better than gritting your teeth.

Try this: five tactics backed by research

Each one links to a study or guide in the list below.

1

Bundle a treat with a chore

Only allow a favorite show or audiobook while you exercise or do a dull task. Katy Milkman calls this temptation bundling.

2

Lock in the choice early

Commitment devices like automatic savings or a stickK contract decide for your future self before temptation shows up.

3

Write an if, then plan

"If I feel like checking my phone at my desk, then I will put it in a drawer." Simple plans like this help you follow through.

4

Add a little friction

Keep the tempting thing out of sight or a few steps away. Wait ten minutes before you give in. Many cravings fade on their own.

5

Meet your future self

Picture yourself in ten or twenty years. Feeling closer to that person makes long term rewards feel more real.
